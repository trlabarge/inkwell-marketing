How Long Should a Chapter Be?

Typical word counts by genre, real novels compared, and how to tell when a chapter is done.

9 min read

The short answer

Most novel chapters run 2,000 to 4,000 words, about 8 to 16 double-spaced manuscript pages. Thrillers often go shorter, around 1,000 to 2,500 words, and epic fantasy longer, 5,000 or more. For an 80,000 word novel, that works out to roughly 20 to 40 chapters. Past that, let each chapter end when its job is done.

How many words is a typical chapter?

There is no required length, but there is a comfortable middle: 2,000 to 4,000 words. That is long enough for something to change and short enough to read in one sitting.

The literary consultancy Jericho Writers describes adult novel chapters in bands, which make a useful way to place your own:

Words How it reads Manuscript pages
Under 1,000Very short. Fine now and then, choppy as an average.Under 4
1,000 to 2,000Short. Quick, propulsive, common in thrillers.4 to 8
2,000 to 4,000Standard. Reads as normal in most genres.8 to 16
4,000 to 5,000Long. Usual in epic fantasy, noticeable elsewhere.16 to 20
Over 5,000Very long. Works as an exception, rarely as a habit.Over 20

The page column uses standard manuscript format, about 250 words to a double-spaced page. A printed paperback holds more, roughly 275 to 375 words a page, so a 3,000 word chapter is about 12 manuscript pages but 8 to 11 printed ones. The word count to pages calculator converts any length.

Reedsy reports a similar picture from 50 novels across genres: averages that mostly fall between 2,000 and 5,000 words.

How long are chapters in each genre?

Genre shifts the middle of the range. Fast genres lean short because a chapter break is a small cliff the reader wants to jump. Immersive genres lean long because readers came to stay a while. These are the ranges commonly cited by writing guides, not rules any publisher enforces:

Genre Typical chapter Why
Thriller, crime, suspense1,000 to 2,500Short chapters and frequent breaks keep the pace high. Some run under 1,000.
Romance2,000 to 4,000Often around 3,000, with chapters that alternate between the two leads.
Science fiction2,500 to 4,000Often around 3,000. Lengthens toward fantasy as the worldbuilding gets heavier.
Young adult2,500 to 4,500Close to adult norms. Voice-led books often run shorter.
Literary fiction3,000 to 5,000Room for interiority and slower scenes.
Fantasy and epic fantasy4,000 to 8,000Long books, big casts, and readers who want to be immersed.
Middle gradeShorter than adultGuides rarely give a firm figure. Measure a few comparable titles (see the note below).

Sources: the bands and genre notes in Jericho Writers, the genre figures in Kindlepreneur’s chapter length guide, and the observation from Reedsy that fantasy chapters tend to run longer and thriller chapters shorter.

Measure the books on your own shelf. The best benchmark is three recent novels in your genre. Count the words on three full pages and average them, multiply by the number of pages in a typical chapter, and you have its length. A page of 330 words and a 12 page chapter is about 4,000 words. Do it for a few chapters in each book and you know what your readers are used to.

How long are chapters in well-known novels?

Two things stand out. First, the range runs from about 1,100 to about 5,000 words, and every one of these books found its readers. Second, chapter length tracks the reading experience, not the book’s size: Kavalier and Clay is more than twice as long as Nineteen Eighty-Four but has shorter chapters, and Patterson’s famously quick chapters are a large part of why his books feel fast.

How many chapters should a novel have?

Divide your target word count by the average chapter length you are aiming for. That is the whole formula:

Number of chapters = total words ÷ average words per chapter. An 80,000 word novel with 3,000 word chapters has about 27 chapters (80,000 ÷ 3,000 = 26.7).

How long should the first chapter be?

Keep it within the usual range for your genre, and if anything, a little on the lean side. The first chapter has more to do than any other (introduce a character, establish a voice, and disturb the ordinary world) and less goodwill to spend while it does it. A reader who has not yet decided to trust you is quicker to put the book down during a long opening.

There is a practical reason too. Literary agents and publishers usually ask for a sample: often the first few pages, sometimes the first three chapters. Ten manuscript pages is about 2,500 words, so in many submissions your first chapter is most or all of what gets read. Check each agent’s own guidelines for the exact sample they want.

While you are drafting, do not worry about any of this. First chapters are usually rewritten more than any other, often once the ending is known. Write it, move on, and trim it in revision. If you are still getting the opening onto the page, the guide to starting a novel covers where a first scene should begin.

How do you know when a chapter should end?

A chapter ends when something has changed and the reader has a reason to keep going. The word count follows from that, not the other way round. A chapter usually ends in one of three places:

  • Right after a turn. A discovery, a decision, a betrayal. The reader closes the chapter knowing the story has shifted.
  • On an open question. A cliffhanger is the strong form: the door opens and we cut away. A softer version leaves an unresolved worry rather than a moment of danger.
  • At a resting point. After a big sequence, a quiet close lets the reader breathe. Used now and then, it makes the next sharp ending land harder.

If a chapter has gone on long, look for the turn in its middle. That is often where it wants to split in two. If it is very short, ask whether anything changed in it. A chapter where nothing shifts usually belongs inside its neighbor.

Inside a chapter, a scene break handles smaller jumps in time, place, or viewpoint without ending the chapter. Many chapters hold a single scene; others hold two or three that build toward the same turn.

Should every chapter be the same length?

No. Readers notice rhythm more than length, and a book where every chapter is exactly 3,000 words can feel mechanical. Aim for a steady average and let individual chapters stretch or shrink with the story.

Variation is a pacing tool. In a novel that averages 3,000 words, a 500 word chapter stands out on the page before a single word is read, which makes it a good place for a shock or a quiet devastating moment. Some writers shorten chapters as the climax approaches, so the book physically speeds up. At the far end, William Faulkner gave one chapter of As I Lay Dying a single five-word sentence, “My mother is a fish.” It works because it is the exception.

What tends not to work is variation without a reason: a 9,000 word chapter in a book of 2,000 word chapters, because the scene simply ran on. To see the rhythm of your chapters, a quick reverse outline with one line and a word count per chapter shows it at a glance.

A chapter length check for revision

Chapter length is a revision question, not a drafting one. Once you have a complete draft, list every chapter with its word count, then work through this:

  1. Find your average. Total words divided by chapters. Compare it with the genre range above.
  2. Flag the outliers. Mark any chapter more than about twice your average, or less than a third of it.
  3. Ask what each outlier is doing. A long chapter that holds a single continuous set piece can stay. A long chapter that holds two turns probably wants splitting.
  4. Check each ending. Read only the last paragraph of every chapter in order. Each one should make you want the next.
  5. Look at the shape. Do chapters shorten as tension rises, or does the climax sit inside the book’s longest, slowest chapter?
  6. Read the first chapter last. Now that you know the whole book, cut anything in the opening that the reader does not need yet.

Questions writers ask about chapter length

How many pages is a chapter?
A chapter of 2,000 to 4,000 words is 8 to 16 pages in double-spaced manuscript format, at about 250 words a page. In a printed paperback, which holds roughly 275 to 375 words a page, the same chapter runs about 5 to 15 pages.
Can a chapter be too short?
A single very short chapter can be one of the strongest moments in a book. William Faulkner gave one chapter of As I Lay Dying a single five-word sentence. The trouble starts when the average drops below about 1,000 words and the book begins to feel choppy, so use very short chapters for effect rather than by default.
Can a chapter be too long?
Yes, if the length is not doing anything. Chapters that regularly pass 5,000 words are unusual outside epic fantasy and some historical fiction. A long chapter often holds two chapters' worth of change, and splitting it at the turn in the middle gives the reader a place to pause and a reason to keep going.
How many chapters is a 50,000 word novel?
About 13 to 25 chapters at the common range of 2,000 to 4,000 words each, or 20 chapters at 2,500 words. Divide your total word count by your average chapter length to find the number for any book.
Should all my chapters be the same length?
No. Most novels vary, and the variation is part of the pacing. Short chapters speed the reader up and long ones let them settle in. Aim for a consistent average, then let each chapter be as long as its job requires.
How many scenes should be in a chapter?
There is no rule. Many chapters hold a single scene. Others hold two or three, separated by a scene break, when they share a viewpoint or build toward the same turn. What matters is that the chapter as a whole changes something.
